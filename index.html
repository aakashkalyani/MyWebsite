About Working Papers Publications Policy Writing Discussions

I study economic growth, innovation, and technological change — with a particular interest in using text as data.

I am an economist at the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. My research uses textual analysis of patents, job postings, and earnings calls to trace how new technologies are created, diffuse across places and workers, and reshape wages, productivity, and inflation.

The Creativity Decline and US Productivity GrowthSubmitted

Aakash Kalyani

The divergence between rising patents and falling productivity growth is explained by a decline in patent creativity — the share of novel technical terminology in patents. A third of the decline is due to a lack of new inventors.

Diffusion of New TechnologiesQuarterly Journal of Economics

Aakash Kalyani, Nicholas Bloom, Marcela Carvalho, Tarek Hassan, Josh Lerner & Ahmed Tahoun

Using textual analysis of patents, job postings, and earnings calls, we track the development and diffusion of new technologies. Development is highly concentrated geographically; hiring then spreads across places and skill levels as technologies mature — a slow process taking around 50 years.
